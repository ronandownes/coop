  are about learning to articulate what you already know, then performing it
  clearly under pressure.

<div class="home-hero">
  <div class="home-hero-kicker">AN INTERVIEW IS A PERFORMANCE</div>
  <div class="home-hero-words">
    <span>ARTICULATE.</span>
    <span>PERFORM.</span>
  </div>
  <p class="home-hero-copy">You already have the course, the projects, the technical knowledge and the experiences. The interview tests whether you can <strong>retrieve them, structure them and say them clearly when it matters.</strong></p>
</div>

<div class="home-three">
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">01</span>
    <strong>Why it feels terrifying</strong>
    <p>You are being watched, judged and asked to produce language in real time. Even when you know the answer, pressure can make the words disappear.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">02</span>
    <strong>What is actually being tested</strong>
    <p>Can you explain? Can you give evidence? Can you connect your course to the job? Can you show judgement, curiosity, independence and fit? The vast majority of candidates prepare answers in advance.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">03</span>
    <strong>Why it can be mastered</strong>
    <p>The themes repeat. The questions repeat. Your evidence repeats. You do not need a new answer every time — you need a small bank of strong answers you can adapt.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="home-performance">
  <div class="home-performance-line">Actors rehearse.</div>
  <div class="home-performance-line">Broadcasters repeat.</div>
  <div class="home-performance-line home-performance-line-strong">Candidates should too.</div>
  <p>A person appearing on several radio or television interviews does not invent a completely new message on every programme. They know their points. They know their examples. They have said them before. <strong>That is interview preparation.</strong></p>
</div>

<div class="home-dont">
  <span>DON'T STRUGGLE FOR WORDS.</span>
  <strong>Build the wording first.</strong>
</div>

<div class="home-skills">
  <div><strong>ARTICULATION</strong><span>Turn technical knowledge into clear spoken language.</span></div>
  <div><strong>STRUCTURE</strong><span>Know where the answer is going before you start speaking.</span></div>
  <div><strong>EVIDENCE</strong><span>Use projects, modules, work and STAR examples instead of empty claims.</span></div>
  <div><strong>CONNECTION</strong><span>Link your course, strengths and interests directly to the company and role.</span></div>
  <div><strong>JUDGEMENT</strong><span>Show that you understand trade-offs, constraints and real workplace problems.</span></div>
  <div><strong>DELIVERY</strong><span>Practise until the important language is available under pressure.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="practice-rule home-practice"><strong>The method:</strong> Develop the answer → edit it → settle it → say it aloud → say it again. Do not keep reinventing it.</div>
