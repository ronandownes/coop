## Flight | 1903 to now



**1903 — powered flight begins**  
The Wright brothers achieved the first sustained, controlled powered flight, using a piston engine to drive twin propellers.

**1910s–1930s — aviation becomes transport**  
Aircraft developed into practical mail, passenger and cargo transport, helped by stronger airframes, better navigation and more reliable engines.

**1940s–1950s — range and altitude increase**  
Pressurised cabins and improved piston engines enabled longer routes, while gas-turbine technology developed rapidly.

**1950s–1970s — the jet age**  
Turbojets and turbofans transformed long-distance travel through greater speed, altitude and range.

**1980s–2000s — market specialisation**  
Wide-body, narrow-body and regional aircraft increasingly served different route structures and passenger markets.

**Today — efficiency matters most**  
Aircraft are selected by balancing range, capacity, fuel burn, emissions, airport constraints, maintenance and operating cost.



## Turboprop | 1940s to now

**Key idea:** A turboprop is a **gas-turbine aircraft** that uses most of its engine power to drive a propeller.

**1940s — emergence**

- Rolls-Royce Dart developed.
- Vickers Viscount flew in 1948.
- Combined turbine power/reliability with propeller efficiency.

**1950s–1970s — regional growth**

- Strong on short routes and smaller airports.
- Examples: Viscount, Fokker F27, HS 748, Twin Otter.

**1980s–1990s — modern generation**

- ATR and Dash 8 families.
- Better engines, avionics, aerodynamics and economics.
- Regional jets added competition.

**2000s–today — efficiency focus**

- Short sectors often favour lower fuel burn and trip cost over maximum speed.
- Turboprops remain valuable for regional routes and constrained airports.

**Remember:** **Propeller ≠ old technology.** Modern turboprops are turbine aircraft optimised for shorter regional flying.

## Regional Aviation | Why the niche exists

- Serves **thin routes** that cannot support large jets.
- Typically uses aircraft around **40–80 seats**.
- Priorities: **low trip cost, frequency, short-runway capability, reliability**.
- Best suited to smaller cities, islands and remote communities.

**Key idea:** regional aviation optimises **economics and access**, not maximum speed.

## ATR | 1981 to now

- **1981:** ATR formed by Aérospatiale and Aeritalia.
- **1984–85:** ATR 42 first flight and entry into service.
- **1988–89:** larger ATR 72 introduced.
- **1990s:** improved **-500** generation.
- **2007 onward:** modern **-600** generation.

**Key idea:** ATR has continuously refined the same core concept — efficient turboprops for short regional routes.

## Aircraft Leasing | The aircraft becomes an asset

- Leasing separates **aircraft ownership** from **airline operation**.
- Lessor: **buys, finances, leases, manages, transitions and remarkets** aircraft.
- Ireland became a major leasing centre from the **1970s onward**.
- Today, a large share of the global commercial fleet is leased.

**Key idea:** a lessor manages the aircraft as a **long-life financial and technical asset**.

## Elix + ADARE | Two complementary foundations

Abelo did not begin from a blank sheet of paper in 2022.

**Elix Aviation Capital — the platform and aircraft**

Elix was established in Dublin in 2013 as a specialist regional-aircraft leasing platform, with a particular focus on turboprops.

Over the following years it built experience across leasing, aircraft transactions and asset management and developed a portfolio including ATR and Dash 8 aircraft.

By the time of the 2022 combination, Abelo described Elix as contributing an established leasing platform and a **65-asset turboprop portfolio**.

Elix also had an existing strategic relationship with ATR. In 2019 it became the launch lessor for the proposed ATR 42-600S short-take-off-and-landing variant.

**ADARE Aviation Capital — the management capability**

ADARE was a much newer platform, established around an experienced regional-aviation management team led by Stephen Gorman, with senior executives who had previously worked in aircraft leasing and regional aviation.

Where Elix brought the operating platform, portfolio and existing customer relationships, ADARE brought a new leadership team and deep experience in areas such as financing, commercial management and regional-aircraft asset management.

That makes the 2022 transaction easier to understand as a **combination of complementary capabilities**, not simply one company changing its name.

## Elix + ADARE | Two foundations

- **Elix:** established turboprop leasing platform + **65-aircraft portfolio**.
- **ADARE:** experienced regional-aviation management team.
- **2022:** merged to create **Abelo**.

## Abelo | 2022 to now

- **2022:** Abelo created; major new ATR commitments followed.
- **2023–24:** continued ATR investment and portfolio growth.
- **2025:** acquired by **Cerberus** from Oaktree.
- **Today:** full-service turboprop leasing and asset management, supporting **60+ aircraft**.

**Key idea:** Elix brought the **platform and fleet**; ADARE brought the **management expertise**.
