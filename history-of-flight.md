## From first flight to commercial aviation

**1903 — powered flight**

The Wright brothers demonstrated sustained, controlled powered flight. Early aviation depended on piston engines driving propellers.

## Piston-engine airliners

Through the first half of the twentieth century, larger piston-engine aircraft made scheduled passenger aviation practical.

The core architecture was still familiar: an engine turned a propeller, and the propeller accelerated air backwards to create thrust.

## The jet age

Turbojet and later turbofan engines transformed long-distance aviation by making much higher speeds and altitudes practical.

Jets became particularly attractive where speed and longer stage length mattered.

## The turboprop

A turboprop also uses a gas turbine, but instead of using most of the exhaust directly for thrust, the turbine drives a propeller.

That makes it a different engineering trade-off rather than an old-fashioned aircraft type.

For shorter regional sectors, lower speeds can be acceptable while fuel efficiency, runway performance and operating economics become more important.

## Regional aviation

Regional aircraft evolved around the problem of moving smaller numbers of passengers efficiently between cities that do not justify a large jet.

This is the context in which modern turboprops such as the ATR family make sense.

## ATR 42 and ATR 72

The ATR family represents a mature modern turboprop design for regional operations.

The key questions are not simply **propeller or jet?**

They are:

- how far is the route?
- how many passengers need to travel?
- what runway and airport constraints exist?
- what fuel and operating costs matter?
- what aircraft utilisation is achievable?
- what residual value and lease economics result?
