## Key rule

**Every serious application gets a tailored CV.**

That does **not** mean inventing a new version of yourself. It means selecting the genuine evidence that is most relevant to the role and describing it in language the employer will recognise.

With AI, producing a carefully tailored application is now much easier. There is little reason to send the exact same generic CV to an aircraft lessor, a corporate service provider, a hospital finance team and a data role.

## Same person, different emphasis

The underlying facts do not change:

- Financial Mathematics at UL
- strong quantitative subjects
- Python, R, MATLAB, Java, SQL and Excel
- statistics, finance, numerical analysis and operations research
- retail and logistics work
- projects and independent learning

What changes is **which evidence comes first and which vocabulary describes it most clearly**.

## Example: aviation leasing

For an aircraft leasing or asset management role, move these ideas forward:

**asset data · lease reporting · financial analysis · valuation · risk · Excel / Power BI · accuracy · commercial awareness · cross-functional work**

A project such as the fleet analysis becomes especially relevant because it proves genuine interest in the sector.

## Example: corporate services / Pivotal

For a CSP or financial-operations role, move these ideas forward:

**accuracy · financial information · cash and transaction awareness · reporting · data quality · client service · responsiveness · governance awareness · process improvement · reliable delivery**

The strongest interview and CV language should connect Erik's real evidence to the employer's real work.

## Example: healthcare / public sector

For a hospital finance or analytics role, foreground:

**financial operations · budgeting awareness · reporting · data analysis · accuracy · teamwork · public-service context · communicating numbers clearly**

## Use the job description as a vocabulary source

Before submitting:

**1. Identify the repeated nouns.**  
Examples: reporting, billing, reconciliation, assets, clients, data, controls.

**2. Identify the repeated verbs.**  
Examples: analyse, support, maintain, prepare, reconcile, communicate, improve.

**3. Identify the behaviours.**  
Examples: accurate, responsive, collaborative, organised, curious.

**4. Match each important word to real evidence.**  
If there is no evidence, do not claim it.

## AI workflow

Give AI the job description and the master CV.

Ask it to:

- extract the employer's recurring language
- identify which existing experiences genuinely match
- reorder bullets by relevance
- tighten wording
- remove material that is less relevant to this application
- flag claims that are unsupported
- generate likely interview questions from the tailored CV

Then read every line manually.

**AI can tailor the presentation. Erik owns the truth of every claim.**

## Final test

Before submitting the CV, Erik should be able to answer:

**“If they point to any line on this CV and ask me to explain it, can I give a clear example without bluffing?”**

If the answer is yes, the CV and interview preparation are aligned.
