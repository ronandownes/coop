## Teamwork

A concrete example comes from **Computer Software II**, where I completed multiple Java assignments with a partner. We divided the work, met in the library to solve problems together, kept each other updated by email when working separately, and then combined and tested the code before submission.

The important part was not simply splitting the task. We had to make two people's work function as one submission, communicate when something changed, and support each other when one part of the solution blocked the other. The module result was **A2**.

**Evidence:** paired software development · shared deadlines · integration and testing · remote and in-person coordination.

## Communication Skills

Technical work only becomes useful when another person can understand the result and act on it. My approach is to identify the audience, decide what they actually need to know, explain the main idea in plain language first, and then add technical detail only where it helps the decision.

That is the same approach I use across this portfolio: aircraft leasing is connected to cash flows, risk and residual value without hiding the commercial meaning underneath mathematical terminology.

**Evidence:** written aviation research · finance explanations · interactive portfolio tools · translating quantitative ideas for different audiences.

## Problem Solving & Analytics

My preferred workflow is:

**define the decision → inspect the data → state assumptions → choose a method → test the result → explain the implication**

The degree gives me several analytical lenses rather than one method for every problem. Probability helps with uncertainty; data analysis helps identify patterns and test claims; numerical analysis provides computational methods; operations research frames constrained decisions; finance and financial mathematics connect future cash flows to value.

I also use programming and data tools to make analysis reproducible rather than relying on a one-off calculation.

### Proprietary systems | What did O'Mahony's teach you about learning business software?

At **O'Mahony's**, I worked inside a real book-order and dispatch workflow supported by **Booksolve** and Excel. My own responsibilities were practical and accuracy-focused: checking that invoices matched the books, checking quantities, identifying who had serviced books when a discrepancy arose, confirming which library an order was going to, and recording how boxes and invoices were grouped for dispatch.

What interests me more now is understanding the system behind those tasks. **Booksolve is not just a stock list.** It can support stock control, purchase-order processing, customer orders and reservations, invoices and credit notes, sales orders, supplier ordering, EDI links, stock availability, returns tracking, reporting, warehouse/distribution workflows and integration with e-commerce and other book-trade services.

I would not claim that I personally operated every Booksolve module. The useful lesson is that a specialist internal system connects several business processes. A data error at one point can affect **stock, fulfilment, billing, reporting and dispatch**, so accuracy in even a simple task matters.

That also changes how I think about **process improvement**. If staff are repeatedly checking information in Excel alongside a core system, I would first understand why that control exists, then ask whether the existing system, a report, a reconciliation check or a small automation could make the process clearer or more reliable without removing an important control.

**Interview cue:** proprietary system · Booksolve · orders · invoices · reconciliation · dispatch · learn the workflow · understand the control · improve carefully

Aircraft leasing is attractive because the mathematics is attached to something physical and operational: an aircraft has a purchase price, lease income, maintenance condition, customer risk, financing cost and future value.

**Power BI** is a specific development priority for this role. My existing foundation is in statistical analysis, programming, data handling and interactive reporting; the next step is to apply that foundation directly in the reporting environment used by the team.

My working standard is straightforward: **be accurate, verify important information, protect confidential data, communicate early when something affects others, and take responsibility for the final output.**
