I’d keep this page to **five distinct questions** and make the R → Python example the concrete evidence that stops the page sounding generic.

## Workload | How do you manage the ebb and flow of work?

I focus on immediate priorities when deadlines are tight. When there is more capacity, I use it for longer-term work: improving a process, developing an ambitious project, or exploring a technology that could be useful later.

priorities | short-term delivery | longer-term goals | improvement

## Learning independently | Can you give an example of learning beyond your coursework?

We used **R for statistics** in college, but I wanted experience with Python as well because of its wider use in data analytics. I therefore recreated my statistical coursework in **both R and Python**, which also let me compare the approaches directly.

R | Python | self-directed learning | portfolio

## New system | How would you learn unfamiliar software?

I would start with the guidance, get a simple task working, and then learn through use. I keep useful notes and ask focused questions when I reach something I cannot resolve myself.

guidance | practice | notes | focused questions

## Keeping skills relevant | How do you decide what to learn next?

I focus on durable skills, but I also watch how the tools used in industry are changing. I experiment with technologies that have a practical application rather than trying to learn every new platform.

durable skills | industry awareness | experimentation | judgement

## AI adaptability | How would you respond if AI changed part of your role?

I would learn where it genuinely improves the work, adapt my skills, and use it responsibly while keeping human judgement and accountability over the result.

adapt | responsible AI | judgement | higher-value work
