## How to learn it | What is the two-pass method?

**Pass 1 — Easy access / recognition:** the wording of the question carries most of the answer. Read it, say the answer, and repeatedly encounter the exact language of the job specification.

**Pass 2 — Retrieval:** the clues disappear. Reconstruct the answer yourself, explain it in ordinary language, and then connect it to your own evidence.

**Progression:** recognise → retrieve → explain → connect → interview answer.

<details>
<summary>Why this works for this page</summary>

The first question is deliberately easy. It is not a weak question: it is the first learning exposure. The paired retrieval question then checks whether the wording has begun to stick.

</details>

## Memory cue | What is slightly different about the two main headings?

**Duties & Responsibilities** requires remembering **both words** as a pair.

**Job Specific Requirements** is easier: because you are already studying a **job specification**, “job specific” is almost automatic. The key retrieval word is **REQUIREMENTS**.

<details>
<summary>Memory line</summary>

**What will I DO? → Duties & Responsibilities.**  
**What must I HAVE? → Requirements.**

</details>

# Duties & Responsibilities

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Yes.**

1. Mechanics of Company Administration  
2. High Standards of Client Care  
3. Accounting, Finance & Operational Support  
4. Company Secretarial Administration

</details>

## DR Overview B — Retrieval | If you got the job, what would your duties and responsibilities be overall?

<details>
<summary>Strong answer</summary>

I would need to **understand how company administration works**, build professional relationships with **intermediaries and internal clients**, provide a high standard of **client care**, support day-to-day **accounting, finance and operational work**, and carry out **company secretarial administration** such as schedules, registers, KYC/CDD and document-signing processes.

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Yes.** The two parts are:

- understand the **mechanics of company administration**
- develop **business-like relationships** with intermediaries and internal clients.

## DR1B — Retrieval | What is the first duty, and what would it mean to you in practice?

<details>
<summary>Strong answer</summary>

The first duty is to understand how company administration works and how the different people around the company interact. In practice, that means learning the processes, knowing who depends on my work, communicating professionally with colleagues and external advisers, and keeping information accurate and moving.

</details>

## DR2A — Easy access | Is DR2 to apply high standards of client care in every dealing with intermediaries and internal clients?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

The second duty is to provide high standards of client care. For me that means being **responsive, accurate, clear, reliable and professional**, following through on requests and making it easy for other people to depend on my work.

# Supporting Concepts from the Extra Slides

## Intermediaries A — Easy access | Is an intermediary someone who operates between parties and helps connect, coordinate or complete a process?

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Yes.** In this context that can include lawyers, banks, auditors, tax advisers, payroll providers, regulators, administration providers and other professional advisers.

</details>

## Intermediaries B — Retrieval | What is an intermediary, and give four examples relevant to Pivotal.

<details>
<summary>Strong answer</summary>

An intermediary is an external person or organisation that operates between parties to help coordinate, advise or complete part of a process. Examples include **lawyers, banks, auditors and tax advisers**.

</details>

## Internal clients | What is the difference between an internal client, an intermediary and an end client?

<details>
<summary>Strong answer</summary>

An **internal client** is a colleague or team inside Pivotal who depends on my work. An **intermediary** is an external professional or organisation helping the process, such as a lawyer, bank or auditor. The **end client** is the company or investor Pivotal is ultimately serving.

</details>

## AP / AR | What do Accounts Payable and Accounts Receivable mean?

<details>
<summary>Strong answer</summary>

**Accounts Payable (AP):** money the company owes suppliers.  
**Accounts Receivable (AR):** money customers owe the company.

</details>

## KYC / CDD | What are KYC and CDD trying to achieve?

<details>
<summary>Strong answer</summary>

**KYC — Know Your Customer:** identify and verify the client.  
**CDD — Customer Due Diligence:** understand the client, ownership, activity and risk so the relationship can be handled compliantly.

</details>

# Final Retrieval

## 60-second synthesis | Tell me what this job is really asking you to do.

<details>
<summary>Model answer</summary>

The role is about being dependable support within a client-service team. I would need to learn how company administration works, communicate professionally with colleagues, clients and intermediaries, and provide accurate accounting and administrative support. That includes records, reconciliations, AP and AR, reporting, company-secretarial tasks, KYC/CDD and document processes. The person they want is organised and detail-oriented, communicates well, is comfortable with Word, Excel and Outlook, takes responsibility and gets involved, and has a genuine interest in financial services and how good work adds value to a client.

</details>
