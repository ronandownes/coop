## Module overview

Fundamentals of Financial Mathematics introduces the mathematical modelling of financial derivatives in discrete-time, arbitrage-free markets. It connects probability, cash flows and hedging directly to financial decision-making.

## Core concepts

- Futures, forwards and options
- Bonds and forward rate agreements
- Arbitrage and put–call parity
- Binomial tree models
- Risk-neutral valuation
- Replication and delta hedging
- Conditional expectation and martingales
- American options
- Simple time-series models

## Key definitions

- **Derivative:** a financial contract whose value depends on another asset or quantity.
- **Arbitrage:** a trading strategy that produces a risk-free profit with no net initial investment.
- **Risk-neutral valuation:** pricing by taking expectations under a probability measure in which appropriately discounted asset prices behave consistently with no-arbitrage.
- **Hedging:** constructing positions that reduce or offset financial risk.

## Important methods / theorems

- No-arbitrage arguments
- Put–call parity
- Binomial pricing
- Replicating portfolios
- Delta hedging
- Conditional expectation

## Explain it simply

The module asks how uncertain future financial payoffs can be **priced consistently and hedged**. Instead of guessing what an option should be worth, it uses probability and no-arbitrage reasoning to build a defensible price.
