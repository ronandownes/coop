## Unknown problem | How do you approach a problem when you do not immediately know the answer?

I break the problem into smaller parts, identify what is known and unknown, check whether I have seen a similar structure before, make reasonable assumptions explicit and test the result. If I am still blocked, I would ask for help with a clear explanation of what I have already tried.

**Recall cue:** Break down → assumptions → test → ask intelligently.

## AI work era | How is work changing in the age of AI?

AI is shifting work away from simply completing routine tasks and toward judgement, problem-solving, communication and oversight. People increasingly need to know how to use AI effectively, check its outputs and apply human judgement where context matters.  
automation|judgement|oversight|human value

## Interdisciplinary work | Why is interdisciplinary thinking becoming more important?

AI makes it easier to connect knowledge across different areas, so many problems are no longer confined to one discipline. Strong workers can combine technical understanding with communication, business awareness and domain knowledge to solve problems more effectively.  
cross-disciplinary|domain knowledge|communication|problem-solving

## AI collaboration | How would you use AI as part of your work?

I would use AI to accelerate research, drafting, analysis or repetitive tasks, while keeping responsibility for the final output. I would check important facts, protect confidential information and make sure the result is appropriate for the actual task.  
productivity|verification|responsibility|confidentiality

## Human judgement | What skills remain important when AI can do more of the technical work?

Judgement becomes more important, not less. People still need to define the problem, recognise when an answer is weak, understand context, make decisions and communicate those decisions clearly to others.  
judgement|context|decision-making|communication

## Adaptability | How would you respond if AI changed part of your role?

I would identify which parts of the role could be automated or improved and focus on learning the skills needed for the higher-value work that remains. I would see the technology as something to adapt to rather than something to ignore.  
adapt|learn|higher-value work|change

## AI limitations | What are the risks of relying too heavily on AI?

AI can produce convincing but incorrect information, miss context or reinforce poor assumptions. I would therefore treat it as a powerful tool rather than an unquestioned authority and verify outputs in proportion to the importance of the task.  
errors|context|verification|proportionate checking

## Problem definition | Why is asking the right question important when using AI?

AI can produce a strong answer to the wrong question, so defining the task clearly is essential. I would clarify the objective, constraints and expected output before deciding how AI could assist.  
objective|constraints|prompting|clarity

## Teamwork | How might AI change the way teams work?

AI can reduce time spent on routine preparation and allow teams to spend more time discussing decisions, solving problems and combining different areas of expertise. It also means teams need clear standards for when AI can be used and how its work is checked.  
collaboration|efficiency|standards|review

## Learning | How do you keep your skills relevant when technology changes quickly?

I would focus on durable skills such as problem-solving, communication and domain knowledge while continuously learning new tools. I would experiment with new technology in practical situations rather than trying to learn every new platform that appears.  
durable skills|continuous learning|experimentation|focus

## Data literacy | Why is data literacy becoming important across different jobs?

AI systems increasingly depend on data, so people need to understand what data represents, where it came from and whether conclusions are justified. Even without being a data specialist, being able to question evidence is becoming an important workplace skill.  
data|evidence|interpretation|critical thinking

## Automation | How would you decide whether a task should be automated?

I would look at how repetitive and predictable the task is, the consequences of an error and whether human judgement is required. Good automation removes unnecessary work without removing appropriate human oversight.  
repetition|risk|judgement|oversight

## Creativity | Does AI reduce the need for creativity?

I think it changes where creativity is applied. AI can generate options quickly, but people still need to recognise what is useful, combine ideas in meaningful ways and decide what fits the real-world context.  
options|selection|context|creativity

## Communication | Why might communication become more valuable in an AI-enabled workplace?

As technology handles more technical production, people still need to explain problems, requirements and decisions to colleagues, customers and stakeholders. Being able to translate technical information into clear language becomes particularly valuable.  
translation|stakeholders|clarity|technical communication

## Responsibility | Who is responsible when work has been produced with AI?

The person or organisation using the tool remains responsible for the work. Using AI should not remove accountability for accuracy, confidentiality, fairness or the consequences of a decision.  
accountability|accuracy|confidentiality|responsibility

## Future of work | What do you think distinguishes someone who will succeed in an AI-enabled workplace?

Someone who can combine domain expertise with adaptability, critical thinking and effective use of technology will be well placed. The strongest approach is not simply knowing how to use AI, but knowing when to use it, when to question it and when human judgement should lead.  
domain expertise|adaptability|critical thinking|judgement
